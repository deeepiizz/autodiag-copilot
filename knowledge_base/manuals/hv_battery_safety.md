# High-Voltage Battery Systems (Hybrid and EV)

> Illustrative procedure for the demo vehicle fleet. Not an OEM service manual.

## Safety first
High-voltage systems operate at several hundred volts DC and can cause fatal injury. Only trained technicians with insulated gloves and Category 0 electrical PPE should work on orange cables or the battery pack. Always follow the manufacturer's de-energising procedure, remove the service disconnect, and wait the specified time before touching HV components.

## Isolation faults (P0AA6)
The battery management system continuously measures insulation resistance between the HV system and chassis. An isolation fault means current could leak to the vehicle body. Do not drive the vehicle. Common causes are coolant intrusion into HV components and damaged HV connectors.

## Pack degradation (P0A80)
P0A80 indicates the battery management system has detected modules or cells drifting apart in voltage or capacity. Review cell voltage data: a spread above roughly 0.3 V between blocks under load suggests weak modules. Also check the battery cooling fan and air inlet, since overheating accelerates degradation.

## Reading pack data
Key signals are pack voltage, pack current, state of charge (SOC) and maximum cell temperature. A pack temperature above 55 C during normal driving points to a cooling problem.
